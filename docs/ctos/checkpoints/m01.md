## The substantive change — provisional vs complete (item 4)

Before M01, `compute_term_grade` returned `None` until every weighted category
had ≥1 scored item, then returned a bare number that the screen rendered
identically whether every item was entered or blanks remained. A teacher could
not distinguish a provisional grade from a complete one. That is the direct
opposite of "provisional values must be visibly provisional."

`ComputedTermGrade` now carries `complete: bool`, propagated through the serde
contract to TypeScript and to both exports. The definition composes with the
existing `None` rule rather than replacing it:

- Some weighted category has zero scored items → `None` → "Not yet available"
- Computable, but ≥1 item in a policy-weighted category has no recorded status → `complete: false` → "(provisional — some scores in this grading period are not yet recorded)"
- Every item in every policy-weighted category recorded → `complete: true`

**Completeness is scoped to policy-weighted categories only.** An unscored item
in a category the policy does not pool cannot change the number, so marking a
grade provisional over it would be the false alarm the flag exists to prevent.

The exported report card and class summary carry the same disclosure the screen
does, and the provisional note takes precedence over the "raised to 60" note —
a floor applied to an incomplete grade is not the fact a teacher reading a
printout needs first. The number is still emitted: disclosure, not suppression.

## Scope decision — item 6

Item 6 as written ("historical **issued** snapshots") is the issued/draft/
amendment pipeline: Working → Draft → Review → Issued → Amendment, with snapshot
immutability. That machinery is M11's acceptance item ("Trusted reporting / SF
pipeline"), and building it early at M01 would be scope creep into a milestone
that owns its own design.

M01's defensible share of item 6 is the **computation-stability** half — the
guarantee that a historical record's grade does not silently move. That is
proved by two tests:

- `compute_term_grade_is_deterministic_across_repeated_calls`
- `compute_term_grade_stays_stable_when_the_default_weight_policy_changes` —
  promotes a different policy to default and asserts the pinned record's grade
  is unchanged

plus migration 42's append-only correction history, which is what makes a
correction auditable rather than overwriting.

The issued-snapshot half remains open and is handed to M11. Recording it here so
the split is a decision, not an omission.
