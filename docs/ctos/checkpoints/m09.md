## Why the scope is what it is

CTOS.md §M09's contract is an outcome, a workflow and five acceptance clauses:

> School planning is constraint-aware, explainable, repairable, and safely
> publishable.
>
> Prepare → Confirm → Lock → Generate → Compare → Repair → Validate → Publish.
>
> Acceptance: independent checker; conflict fixtures; stale-generation
> publication rejection; atomic publication; version-consistent
> teacher/section/room views.

Reading that against the repository rather than designing blind: ADR-0039 had
built `teaching_assignments` and `schedule_meetings` and had deliberately
deferred the generator as a hypothesis. The constraint inputs the generator
needs — a bell grid, teacher availability, a room registry, per-subject weekly
requirements — did not exist. So the milestone's real work was the _Lock_: a
school's constraints had to become one comparable set before any of the five
clauses could mean anything.

Two decisions followed from the acceptance list rather than from preference:

1. **The checker is a separate module that reads the database.** "Independent
   checker" is the first clause, and a checker that reused the generator's own
   interval bookkeeping would be the generator grading itself. `check.rs`
   re-derives every conflict from placements and inputs it reads fresh — which
   is also what makes a human repair validatable, the "Repair → Validate" half
   of the workflow.
2. **Every refusal is a value, not an error.** `PublishOutcome` has five
   variants and `GenerationOutcome` has three; a stale or violating plan is the
   workflow speaking, and the screen switches on `outcome` the same way
   `CreateMeetingOutcome`'s callers already do. This is why the tests assert on
   `matches!` over an `Ok(...)`, not on `Err`.

Deliberately out of scope: curriculum/term applicability. Of §M09's eleven
required constraints it is the one with nothing to bind to — `subjects` carry
no term and no grade-level applicability, so a rule would have to be invented
along with its data. That is recorded as a parked gap, not papered over.

## The change

**1. The constraint inputs (migration 46).** Five additive tables:
`schedule_settings` (one row per school, materialized with national-policy
defaults — 07:30–17:00, five days, 50-minute periods, a 10-minute passing
buffer, and DepEd Order No. 005 s. 2024's six-hour daily classroom-teaching
ceiling as `max_daily_teaching_minutes`), `teacher_unavailability` (blocked
windows, not free ones, so a fully-available teacher costs nothing to
represent), `schedule_rooms` (with an `is_lab` flag), `subject_schedule_requirements`
(the demand side), and the plan schema itself: `schedule_plans` plus
`schedule_plan_meetings`.

**2. The Lock.** `scheduling/constraints.rs` loads every input for a school
into one struct and `fingerprint` SHA-256s it. The fingerprint is stamped on
the plan at creation and recomputed at publication; a mismatch is the
"stale-generation publication rejection" clause — a plan built against last
term's assignments can never silently become this term's live schedule.

**3. The Generate step.** A greedy most-constrained-first search with a step
budget, landing in exactly one of three states: `Valid`, `Impossible` (with an
`ImpossibilityProof` — required weekly minutes against available weekly
minutes, so the refusal is arithmetic, not a timeout excuse), or `Stopped`
(with the steps used and the assignments still unplaced). The plan exists in
all three; a partial timetable alongside a proof is still worth repairing.

**4. The independent checker.** `scheduling/check.rs` reads placements and
inputs fresh and re-derives eleven violation kinds — teacher, section and room
conflicts; teacher unavailability; daily and weekly overload; missing passing
buffers; shared learners (derived from actual `section_memberships` overlap);
requirement shortfalls; unknown rooms; and `legacyConflict`, which validates a
draft against the school's hand-created meetings rather than deleting them.
Each violation names the constraint and the people and times involved, because
an unexplained rejection is not repairable.

**5. The Publish step.** `schedule_plan::publish` is the only `&mut Connection`
function in the module. Lock again, Validate independently, then one
`transaction()` that supersedes the previous published plan, replaces only the
meetings that plan owned, writes this plan's meetings, and publishes — either
fully committing or fully rolling back. Meetings the school created by hand
(`plan_id IS NULL`) are untouched.

**6. The boundary.** 18 commands in `commands/schedule_planning.rs`. Writes go
through `authorize_capability_with_actor(Capability::ManageTeachingAssignments)`,
so the school **and the actor** are re-derived from the session and the actor
is stamped on the publication; reads go through `require_active_session`. No
command accepts a client-supplied school id.

**7. The frontend.** The TS port (`domain/schedule-planning.ts` mirroring the
Rust shapes field-for-field, including every tagged-enum encoding serde uses on
the wire), the port, the thin application service, the Tauri adapter, and
`SchedulePlannerScreen.tsx` laid out as the workflow itself: Prepare (the
eight-knob grid form), Generate, Compare and repair (the placements table with
Remove), Validate, Publish. Wired in `composition.ts` only, per the architecture
guard, and reached from a "Plan the whole timetable…" action on the Sections
screen plus a nav entry.

## Not claimed

- **Curriculum/term applicability is not enforced.** Of §M09's eleven required
  constraints this is the one with no checker rule, because `subjects` carry no
  term and no grade-level applicability for a rule to bind to. The ten other
  constraints are implemented and tested. A curriculum table is a later
  milestone's decision, not this one's invention.
- **The generator is greedy, not optimal.** Most-constrained-first with a step
  budget. It proves _impossibility_ arithmetically and reports `Stopped` when
  the budget is spent; it does not prove optimality, and no schedule produced
  here is claimed to be the best possible one.
- **The browser axe gate does not traverse the planner screen.** `npm run
quality:ui` drives the dev preview, which cannot reach the new tab. The
  screen is axe-checked in jsdom by its own `keeps the planner accessible` test
  — structure and ARIA only, explicitly not contrast or layout, since jsdom has
  no layout engine. Both runners ran; neither is claimed for the other.
- **No native Windows or Android evidence this milestone.** The Rust suite
  exercises the new migration, repositories, generator and checker against the
  encrypted in-memory device database; the installed packages were not built or
  exercised. Native claims remain where they were.
- **No hand-created meeting was used to seed any fixture**, and no published
  plan was assumed: every plan in the test suite is generated by the pipeline
  or hand-built in an in-memory database. No production school data exists in
  this repository and none was invented.
